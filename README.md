# SalMoTracker
Code and supporting information concerning the survey of diversity and heterogeneity of mobilizable elements and antimicrobial resistance genes within and across host and non-host adapted serovars of poultry Salmonella isolates.

## Project overview and current status

This project plan to reflect the analysis that has now been completed and the next genomic phase of the study. The project has evolved into a two-stage framework: first, 
a temporal surveillance analysis of Salmonella serovar composition in U.S. poultry-associated isolates; second, targeted genomic analysis of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) and 
mobile genetic elements (MGEs) in the serovars and lineages identified as epidemiologically important.

| Phase | Status | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 - Temporal serovar surveillance | Completed / quality-control ongoing | Characterize year-by-year serovar composition in preharvest and postharvest poultry-associated isolates. |
| Phase 2 - Preharvest vs postharvest comparison | Next analytical step | Identify serovars enriched before versus after harvest and evaluate temporal shifts. |
| Phase 3 - AMR/MGE genomic analysis | Planned | Profile resistance genes and plasmid/integron/transposon-associated signals in selected serovars/SNP clusters. |
| Phase 4 - Integrated interpretation | Planned | Link serovar, year, source stage, SNP lineage, AMR genotype, and MGE profile. |

## Refined Research Questions

### Primary Research Question

> How do *Salmonella enterica* serovar composition, antimicrobial resistance profiles, and mobile genetic element patterns vary over time and between preharvest and postharvest poultry-associated isolates in the United States?

### Current Sub-Questions

1. Which *Salmonella* serovars dominate preharvest and postharvest poultry-associated NCBI Pathogen Detection isolates, and how do their proportions change from 2016–2017 through 2024–2025?
2. Which serovars show persistent, increasing, declining, newly emerging, or sporadic temporal patterns?
3. Do major serovars differ in relative representation between preharvest and postharvest sources?
4. Are temporal shifts in important serovars, especially Infantis, Kentucky, Enteritidis, Typhimurium, and Schwarzengrund, associated with distinct AMR gene profiles?
5. Which plasmids, integrons, transposons, insertion sequences, or other MGEs are associated with the detected AMR genes?
6. Do different SNP clusters or serovars carry different MGE profiles, suggesting clonal expansion versus independent horizontal acquisition?
7. How well do AMR genotypes correspond to available AST phenotypes?

## Why This Study Matters

Poultry-associated *Salmonella* is relevant to food safety, animal health, and human health. AMR genes can spread not only through expansion of resistant clones but also through horizontal transfer on plasmids, integrons, transposons, and related MGEs.

Separating temporal serovar trends from genomic resistance mechanisms allows the project to ask both epidemiologic and mechanistic questions: **which lineages are becoming more common, and what genetic features may help explain their persistence or spread?**

### Key Contributions

- Provides a longitudinal view of serovar composition rather than a single cross-sectional snapshot.
- Separates preharvest and postharvest source groups to examine possible stage-specific patterns.
- Creates a rational basis for selecting serovars and SNP clusters for deeper genomic analysis.
- Supports a One Health interpretation by linking food-animal surveillance, AMR, and horizontal gene transfer.
